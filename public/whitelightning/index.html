THE FULL RUNDOWN
Hang the tail out to match every curve. Green is safe but slow; ride the needle toward the edge and she pulls up to half again harder. The packed dark middle of the road holds her steady; the pale loose stuff toward the edges rattles the needle. The ridge itself rolls under you: downgrades hand you free speed, climbs bleed it off, and the dash reads it plain: red arrows climbing, green arrows dropping, elevation in feet alongside. A painted plank warns of the curve ahead before you're in it. Lantern-lit barns along the ridge are drop points: cross the chalk line and the keeper opens his counter. You can't stop to sell with the law on your tail, so when a revenuer's chasing you blow past the drop — the barn, Atlanta, even the hideout — and the run past the lantern kicks in a boost to help you shake him. No sale that drop, but a full head of steam. You only fill the trunk at the hideout — load her once and that shine rides with you the whole run. At every barn you decide how many jars to drop off for cash, and the price climbs the harder the road you took and the deeper you carry it toward Atlanta. Sell your whole untouched load in one shot for a full-case bonus, so holding out for Atlanta pays the most — but a broken jar or a busted run down there costs you everything back there. Every third drop rolls you into a town like Atlanta, then three drops carry you back to the hideout. Atlanta's rail yard is the only place to buy V8 parts; the hideout is where you install them and reload the trunk. Load heavier and she hauls more cash but drives like it. The revenuers race the same road behind you, faster on the straights, weaker in the curves. Bumper to bumper and it's over. But open a big enough lead and hold it — through the curves where he has to lift — and he loses your dust and drops the chase. The tighter the road, the quicker he loses you: a hairpin stretch like the Horseshoe shakes the law far sooner than the open Moonshine Mile. The gap bar reads SHAKING HIM when you're pulling clear.
